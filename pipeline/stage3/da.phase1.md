## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): From an adversarial stance, I read this as asking whether the procedure behind the work could be reproduced and whether its design choices would survive a hostile replication attempt. For a survey, that means asking whether the way sources were found, kept, compared, and weighted is stated clearly enough that someone else with a different prior would arrive at the same corpus and the same comparisons. It is mandatory and belongs to the methodology seat, so I will not score it, but I will flag any coherence failure that comes from it.

D2 (domain_accuracy): Adversarially, this dimension asks whether the factual base is accurate. Are cited results reported faithfully, is prior work presented in the form its authors would recognise, and is technical terminology used correctly? An argument resting on a misreported result fails even if its logic is valid. It is mandatory and belongs to the domain seat, so I will not score it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This is the dimension I own. It asks whether the central thesis can withstand the strongest available counter-argument. I will test whether the claims follow from the evidence presented, whether the evidence was selected to favour a conclusion fixed in advance, whether generalisations go beyond the number and kind of cases that support them, and whether the thesis contradicts itself between sections. I will also check whether alternative explanations that fit the same evidence equally well are acknowledged and answered. Fallacies count against this dimension only when they weaken the central argument, not when they are incidental.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): Read adversarially, this asks whether claims addressed to adjacent fields actually hold there or are just asserted to transfer. It also asks whether definitions are precise enough that a reader from another discipline could not reasonably read them a different way. It is high priority and belongs to the perspective seat, so I will not score it.

D5 (writing_and_structure): Adversarially, this asks whether the organisation and presentation help the reader check the argument or hide its weak points, for example by placing qualifications where they are easy to miss or by using tables that frame comparisons selectively. It is normal priority and belongs to the editor seat, so I will not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): From a challenger's view, this asks whether the claimed contribution is really original and significant for the intended readership, or whether it restates known material under a new framing. It also asks whether the work meets what the configured venue requires. It is mandatory and belongs to the editor seat, so I will not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the central thesis survives its strongest counter-argument; whether the cases cited are representative rather than selectively chosen; whether generalisations are proportionate to the size and kind of the evidence; whether claims stay consistent across sections; whether rival explanations of the same evidence are acknowledged and addressed.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing claim that the cited evidence does not support, or a credible rival explanation left unaddressed that would overturn a main conclusion if accepted, where repairing it would require reworking the argument rather than editing text.
what_triggers_warn: Local overgeneralisation, hedging that is missing or inconsistent, or counterexamples that are acknowledged but dismissed too quickly, where the central thesis still stands once these are corrected.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is internally contradictory, or depends on a fallacy such as circular reasoning or systematic cherry-picking without which it collapses, so that no revision short of a new thesis could save it.
