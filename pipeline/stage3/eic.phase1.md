## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): From an editorial standpoint, this asks whether the way the work was carried out would survive scrutiny by peers in the field. For a survey, the contract's hint redirects rigor away from statistics toward how the reviewed corpus was assembled, whether the search is transparent enough to be repeated, whether the rules used to compare approaches are stated and applied consistently, and whether stronger evidence is weighted above weaker evidence. I do not score this dimension, but I will route any editorial concern about it to the owning seat.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): This dimension concerns whether what the manuscript asserts matches the current body of domain evidence, whether cited prior work is described faithfully, and whether technical terms and reported results are used correctly. As editor I rely on the domain seat for this judgment and will not substitute my own reading of individual designs for theirs.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate with methodology also eligible): This asks whether the central thesis holds together, whether the evidence presented actually carries the claims made, and whether any reasoning errors undermine the main line of argument. Editorially, a survey whose synthesis does not follow from its own comparisons is a serious problem, but scoring belongs to the eligible seats.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by the perspective seat): This concerns whether readers from neighbouring fields can follow the framing and definitions, and whether any claims that reach across disciplines are backed up. A block here forces a major revision under F4. I do not score it.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by me): This is the editor's own check that the manuscript is well organised, clearly written, uses figures and tables effectively, and follows the conventions expected by its target venue. Because the configured target is the author's course brief, venue conventions here mean the brief's expectations on length, section structure, and use of comparative tables. A warn or block here, being a normal-priority dimension, leads at most to a minor revision under F5.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by me): This is the editor's central judgment of whether the manuscript belongs at the configured venue and offers something original and significant for that readership. Per the contract amendment, the venue is the author's course rubric, so fit means satisfying the brief's required elements and weighting, and contribution means a survey that goes beyond summary to deliver its own critical comparison and synthesis. Because it is mandatory, a block triggers major revision under F2 and a fatal block triggers rejection under F1.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: A logical section order that a reader can navigate, clear prose, comparative tables that are legible and actually referenced in the text, length within the brief's page range excluding references, and consistent citation and formatting conventions.
what_triggers_block: Organisation or exposition broken enough that a reader cannot follow the comparison, such as missing or unusable comparative tables, length far outside the brief's page range, or sections whose order prevents the synthesis from being understood.
what_triggers_warn: Localised clarity or presentation problems, such as tables not referenced in prose, uneven section depth, minor length overshoot, inconsistent terminology or formatting, that hamper reading without preventing comprehension.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: Coverage of every required brief element (state of the art, competing approaches, key research questions, trade-off and limitation comparison, open challenges, critical synthesis), at least twenty mostly recent peer-reviewed references, comparative tables, discussion of performance, efficiency, dependability, security, or predictability, and a synthesis that adds the author's own critical judgment beyond summarising sources.
what_triggers_block: One or more required brief elements is absent or only nominal, the reference base falls short of the brief's count or recency expectation, or the work is predominantly descriptive summary with no substantive critical comparison or synthesis of its own.
what_triggers_warn: All required brief elements are present but one or more is thin, the critical-systems discussion covers only a narrow subset of the listed properties, or the synthesis is present but its added value over the cited surveys is modest or under-argued.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript is not a comparative survey on the stated topic at all, or it fails the brief so fundamentally (for example, no comparison of competing approaches and no synthesis together) that revision could not bring it within the configured venue's scope.
