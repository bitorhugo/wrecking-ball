## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): From where I sit as an outside reader, this asks whether the way the work was assembled can be trusted and repeated. For a survey that means a visible, defensible route from search to corpus to comparison, so that a reader from a neighbouring field could rebuild the evidence base and see why each source carries the weight it is given. I do not score this dimension.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat): This concerns whether statements about the core field match what its own evidence actually shows, whether earlier work is reported faithfully, and whether technical vocabulary and reported results are used without error. Mistakes here would also mislead adjacent-field readers who cannot check them independently. I do not score this dimension.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate with methodology also eligible): This asks whether the central claim holds together from start to finish, whether each conclusion is actually carried by the evidence cited for it, and whether any reasoning slip undercuts the main line. I do not score this dimension.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by my seat): This is my dimension. It asks whether someone trained in a neighbouring discipline (for me, dependable, secure and real-time systems) can follow the framing, finds terms defined rather than assumed, and can see what the conclusions mean for their own work. It also asks that any claim reaching beyond the home field is backed by reasoning or sources from the field it reaches into, rather than asserted.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by the editor seat): This covers how the document is organised, how clearly it explains itself, whether figures and tables do their job, and whether it follows the expected conventions of the target outlet. I do not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor seat): This asks whether the work belongs at its intended destination (here, as configured, the author-supplied requirements) and whether it adds something new and meaningful for that audience. I do not score this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Explicit definitions of terms that adjacent-field readers need; correct and consistent use of predictability vocabulary such as WCET, bounded latency and determinism; any dependability or security discussion stating an explicit threat model with attacker capabilities, assets and trust boundaries; cross-field claims supported by sources from the field being invoked; implications for critical or security-sensitive deployments that go beyond listing open gaps to give actionable analysis.
what_triggers_block: A central cross-disciplinary claim (security, dependability or timing predictability) is asserted without supporting evidence or with misused core terminology, such that an adjacent-field reader would draw a materially wrong conclusion.
what_triggers_warn: Cross-field framing is broadly sound but has undefined key terms, a missing or informal threat model, loose use of predictability language, or adjacent-field implications presented only as a gap list without analysis.
